# Localisation

The app is written in English and Slovenian. Everything a reader sees comes from one of three places:

| What | Where | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Interface and story sentences | `src/i18n/messages/<lang>.json` | ICU MessageFormat (plurals, selects), flat keys |
| Names: country columns, states, events, eras | `src/i18n/names/<lang>.json` | Plain strings by id |
| Years, ranges, shares, areas, lists, time phrases | `src/i18n/format.ts` | Code, per language |

English is the source catalogue: `MessageKey` is typed from `en.json`, and a unit test fails if another catalogue misses a key, adds one, uses a placeholder English does not have, or fails to parse.

Text written by the data build — notes on culture areas, reasons a column is missing, quotations from Wikipedia — stays in English and is marked `lang="en"` so screen readers and hyphenation treat it correctly. Quotations are never translated: they are what the cited revision says.

## How the reader's language is chosen

`?lang=` in the address, then the reader's last choice (stored), then the browser's languages, then English. The **EN / SL** switch in the header changes it and updates `?lang=` so links keep the language. `<html lang>` follows it.

## Slovenian conventions

- Years: `27 pr. n. št.`, `476 n. št.`, `1500`; ranges `300–200 pr. n. št.`; centuries `16. stoletje`; decades `1620. leta`; approximate `ok. 1200`. No-break spaces inside the abbreviations.
- Numbers and shares: `207.000 km²`, `62 %`. Lists: `A, B in C`. Quotation marks: `»…«`.
- Plurals have four forms (one, two, few, other): `1 država`, `2 državi`, `3 države`, `5 držav`.
- Sentences that insert a name avoid gender and case agreement with it: "ozemlje, ki ga danes obsega {lane}", "v rokah države {ruler}", "Ta oblast je segala…".
- Country columns use CLDR names (`Intl.DisplayNames`), with overrides for split and merged columns and for everyday forms (ZDA, DR Kongo, Vzhodni Timor).
- Story text translated from English follows the newspaper style sheet in [research/slovenian-newspaper-style.md](research/slovenian-newspaper-style.md): past-tense narration, no calques, Slovenian exonyms, lowercase event names in running text.

## Names of states, events and eras

The data's names are English. Names in another language are a data snapshot, like the rest of the cited layers:

```bash
cd data && python3 tools/fetch_names.py . sl     # English article → language link; else Wikidata label
cd .. && node scripts/gen-names.mjs sl           # writes the states, events and eras into src/i18n/names/sl.json
```

A name is the article's title in that language without a bracketed disambiguation, else the Wikidata label with a capital initial (Slovenian labels are in dictionary form, "bitka pri Nanchangu"). Two records with different English names never get the same name: that happens when the source links a state to a broader article (Cliopatria links "Carolingian Empire" to *West Francia*) or when one article covers two states. The shared name stays with the record whose English name is its own article's title (and, among several, whose Wikidata label confirms it); the others take their own label when no other record has it, else keep their English name. A unit test checks the result.

Slovenian coverage (snapshot of 2026-10-21): 1,064 of 1,696 states, 1,488 of 4,086 events and 13 of 27 eras with an article of their own. Without a name, the chart shows the English one; a story then takes the title of the reader's own Wikipedia article as its headline when one exists.

### Corrections

A snapshot name that names the wrong thing is corrected under `overrides` in `names/<lang>.json` (for example the 2016 Trans-Pacific Partnership, which Wikidata labelled as its 2005 predecessor). `gen-names.mjs` applies them after the snapshot, so a rebuild keeps them. Translating the Americas found three more Slovenian corrections: the invasion of Grenada dated 1993 instead of 1983, the War of Jenkins' Ear named after a "Jenkinson", and *Rio de Janeira* for the declined *Ria de Janeira*. The Middle East found six: *Nordjemen* (German) for North Yemen, *Novo asirsko/babilonsko kraljestvo* for the Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian empires, the sieges of Acre as *Ake*, English spellings for Jaffa and Deir ez-Zor, and the 2016 battle for Fallujah counted as the fourth instead of the third. Anatolia and North Africa found one: the sack of *Armorij* for Amorij (Amorium). Iberia and the British Isles found one: *Vojna žajncev* for the Reapers' War (*žanjcev*). France and the Low Countries found one: *Bitka pri Wssembourgu* for the Battle of Wissembourg. Central Europe found three: *Hanoversko* for the Kingdom of Hanover, *poljsko-avatrijska vojna* for the Polish–Austrian War and *bitka pri Wipfnu* for the Battle of Wimpfen.

## Translated summaries

Where the reader's language has no Wikipedia article, the drawer shows a stored translation of the English summary, marked as an AI translation ([pattern](patterns/story-drawer.md#ai-translations)). Translations are data, built in four steps:

```bash
node scripts/translation-worklist.mjs sl                       # data/translations/sl/_worklist.json: what to translate, by priority
cd data && python3 tools/fetch_translation_sources.py . sl oceania   # re-checks each article, snapshots the English intro and its revision
cd .. && python3 -I scripts/translation-chunks.py sl oceania drafts oc   # drafts/oc-01.txt …: untranslated units, 40 a chunk, with glossary names
# translate each chunk with the slovene-newspaper-translation skill into drafts/oc-NN.json
node scripts/merge-translations.mjs sl oceania drafts/oc-01.json --partial --translator "…"   # checks, then writes data/translations/sl/oceania.json
```

- A unit is one English article, translated once and used in every region it appears in. An era that is a section of a longer article ("Woodland period", section "Early Woodland period") is its own unit, keyed `title#section`; the drawer looks it up the same way and cites the section. The worklist orders units by what readers see first: eras, the states that lead a column at the Empires level, other states, detail-only states, then events by their number of Wikipedia editions.
- The merge refuses a batch with a missing unit, a different number of paragraphs, a number of the English missing from the translation, or a calque the style sheet bans. `npm test` repeats the checks. A large region is translated in parts: `--partial` accepts a batch that is not complete yet, keeps the units already written and adds the new ones, so each part can be committed. A region too large for one pass is split by its column groups: `fetch_translation_sources.py … --groups levant,arabia` snapshots only the units shown in those columns, and a unit shared by two parts goes with the first one translated.
- Wikipedia editors rename section headings (the Woodland sections' dates changed in 2026). The fetch tool and the drawer both fall back to the heading without its bracketed dates.
- Each unit keeps the English text it was made from and its revision, so the drawer can show the original and the source can cite the exact revision.
- The style: [research/slovenian-newspaper-style.md](research/slovenian-newspaper-style.md).
- In a definition sentence the verb agrees with the subject, not with the noun after it: *Bitka pri Tangi je bila neuspešen vdor*, *Obleganje Akre je bilo protinapad*, *Padec Faludže je bil bitka*. A sweep on 23 October 2026 corrected 189 summaries in Africa & Middle East, the Americas and Oceania that had let the verb follow the noun after it. A neuter subject needs *je bilo* even when the noun after it is masculine or feminine: *Obstreljevanje Ancone je bilo pomorski spopad*; where that reads badly, an apposition does it (*Premirje v vili Giusti ali padovansko premirje, premirna konvencija z Avstro-Ogrsko, je …*).
- A name the glossary holds is used in running text too, not only as the headline: *dirka do morja*, not a fresh *tek k morju*.
- Where a Slovenian form is held by the glossary, the glossary wins over a form that sounds more familiar: *Kraljevina dveh Sicilij* (the Slovenian Wikipedia title), not *Obeh Sicilij*.
- Greek names in *-as* decline as Slovenian *a*-stems, as the Pravopis has *Leonida -e*: *Epaminonda, pod vodstvom Epaminonde*; *Pelopida, Pelopide*.
- Peace treaties are *mir v X* (*mir v Stolbovu*, *mir v Bučaču*) unless the glossary or established usage gives another name (*Tilsitski mir*, *zadrski mir*); alliances and other treaties are *X-ska pogodba* (*Georgijevska pogodba*). Ukrainian and Russian places keep the forms Slovenian media use (*Lugansk*, *Doneck*, *Harkov*, *Severski Donec*), and the glossary is corrected where it strays (*Kampanja v Luganski oblasti*).
- Names of two countries joined by a hyphen decline in both parts, as one would say them: *Danska-Norveška, Danske-Norveške, z Dansko-Norveško*. Danish kings take the established forms *Kristjan* and *Friderik* (the living king *Frederik X.*); Vytautas is *Vitovt*; the historical duchies are *Šlezvik* and *Holštajn*, while the modern German state stays *Schleswig-Holstein*.
- Chinese names follow the Slovenian transcription of pinyin that the glossary already uses (*Džin, Čing, Ljao, Juan*; *Šinjiang*, *Kangši*), and pinyin, Wade–Giles, Wylie and other romanizations are dropped while the original script stays. Established forms win: *Džingis-kan*, *Kublaj*, *Songcen Gampo*; the Caspian Sea is *Kaspijsko jezero*.
- A name a later summary settles is carried back to earlier mentions: once *Kališki mir* and *mir v Melnu* became headlines, the Raciążek and Golub War summaries were changed to match.

## Adding a language

1. Add it to `LOCALES` in `src/i18n/locales.ts` (id, own name, BCP 47 tag, Wikipedia edition).
2. Copy `messages/en.json` to `messages/<lang>.json`, translate, and register it in `CATALOGUES` in `src/i18n/i18n.ts`.
3. Add its year words and plural rule for durations to `WORDS` in `src/i18n/format.ts`, and its time phrases to `when()`.
4. Generate country names from CLDR (see the note in `names/sl.json`), then fetch and generate the other names as above.
5. `npm test` checks the catalogue; read a few stories of each kind in the new language before release.
